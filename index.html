Towards Saner Deep Image Registration

ICCV 2023

Bin Duan, Ming Zhong, Yan Yan
Illinois Institute of Technology, Chicago, IL, USA
Paper arXiv Code BibTeX

Abstract

With recent advances in computing hardware and surges of deep-learning architectures, learning-based deep image registration methods have surpassed their traditional counterparts, in terms of metric performance and inference time. However, these methods focus on improving performance measurements such as Dice, resulting in less attention given to model behaviors that are equally desirable for registrations, especially for medical imaging. This paper investigates these behaviors for popular learning-based deep registrations under a sanity-checking microscope. We find that most existing registrations suffer from low inverse consistency and nondiscrimination of identical pairs due to overly optimized image similarities. To rectify these behaviors, we propose a novel regularization-based sanity-enforcer method that imposes two sanity checks on the deep model to reduce its inverse consistency errors and increase its discriminative power simultaneously. Moreover, we derive a set of theoretical guarantees for our sanity-checked image registration method, with experimental results supporting our theoretical findings and their effectiveness in increasing the sanity of models without sacrificing any performance.

Contributions

  1. We find that despite better performance such as Dice, most models produce non-smooth transformations and are less aware of sanity errors due to over-optimization on image similarities.
  2. We propose two novel sanity checks for the registration model training and derive the corresponding theoretical foundations.
  3. Our sanity checks not only help reduce the sanity errors of existing models but also assist them in producing more regular maps without diffeomorphic add-ons.
  4. Our sanity checks are model-agnostic. They can be deployed to various models and are only needed during training, so they generate no side effects for inference.
  5. Experiments on IXI, OASIS, and BraTSReg verify our findings and show on-par or better performance.

Method

Training a sanity-checked model. Ⓢ denotes spatial warping. A shared model predicts displacement maps in both directions. The self-sanity check (ℒself) forces zero displacement for identical pairs (ga→a = 0), increasing discriminative power. The cross-sanity check (ℒcross) enforces a relaxed inverse consistency between the forward and back-projected maps, reducing inverse-consistency errors. Both are combined with the standard similarity (ℒsim) and regularization (ℒreg) losses, and are needed only at training time.

Self-sanity check

Restricts the mapping to output zero displacement for identical pairs, so the model stops hallucinating motion where there is none — improving discrimination of identical pairs.

Cross-sanity check

A relaxed, data-driven inverse-consistency constraint with error tolerance (α,β). It allows occlusions/topological changes while keeping forward/backward maps consistent, with a proven upper bound on the error.

Theoretical guarantees

A unique minimizer for a single image pair, and a provable upper bound between the optimal and sanity-checked minimizers that guides the choice of loss weights.

Key Theorem

Theorem 1 — Relaxed registration via cross-sanity check.

An ideal symmetric registration meets $\varphi^{a\rightarrow b}\circ\varphi^{b\rightarrow a}=\mathit{id}$. Then, a cross-sanity-checked registration is a relaxed solution to this ideal registration, satisfying

$$\big\lVert\, g^{a\rightarrow b}+\tilde{g}^{b\rightarrow a}\,\big\rVert_2^2 \;<\; \frac{\beta(2-\alpha)\,N}{1-\alpha},$$

where $0<\alpha<1$, $\beta>0$, and $N$ is the total number of voxels.

Why it matters. Strict inverse consistency forces $g^{a\rightarrow b}+\tilde{g}^{b\rightarrow a}=0$ everywhere, which is impractical when real correspondences are missing (e.g. occlusions or tumor regions). Theorem 1 shows the cross-sanity check instead admits a whole enclosed set of valid solutions — a ball of radius $\sqrt{\beta(2-\alpha)N/(1-\alpha)}$ — controlled by the tolerance parameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$. This relaxed constraint keeps forward/backward maps consistent while staying close to the optimal minimizer (a loyalty bound of $2\lambda_c(1-\alpha)\beta N$), which is exactly why the sanity checks improve regularity and inverse consistency without sacrificing Dice.

Eliminating Errors During Training

The two sanity checks are folded into a single training objective alongside the usual similarity and regularization terms:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\text{sim}} \;+\; \lambda_{r}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} \;+\; \lambda_{s}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{self}} \;+\; \lambda_{c}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cross}}.$$

The cross-sanity loss is computed through a binary violator mask $\mathcal{M}$ that flags every voxel breaking the cross-sanity check, so the penalty is applied only to the points that are still inconsistent (visualized as occlusion masks). As optimization proceeds, the model drives these violators toward zero — literally erasing the sanity errors over the course of training.

Mask evolution during training. White pixels are voxels violating the cross-sanity check (top: $\mathcal{M}^{m\rightarrow f}$, bottom: $\mathcal{M}^{f\rightarrow m}$). From left to right (training progress), the violators steadily vanish — the self-/cross-sanity errors are eliminated without extra cost at inference time.

Results

Qualitative comparisons on IXI. Columns: moving/fixed pair, warped grids in both directions, and the self-sanity error (SSE) and cross-sanity error (CSE) maps (maximum value marked top-left). Our sanity-checked models produce more regular deformation maps and dramatically lower sanity errors.

Comparisons on the OASIS validation set. Our method produces more regular maps for all input image pairs while matching state-of-the-art Dice.

Across IXI, OASIS, and BraTSReg, adding the sanity checks reduces self-/cross-sanity errors and folded voxels while keeping Dice on par with — or better than — strong baselines such as VoxelMorph and TransMorph.
